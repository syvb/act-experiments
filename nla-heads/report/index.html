What the layer-20 NLA says about single attention heads

I took the output of individual attention heads (each head's slice of the attention output, projected through its block of WO into the 3584-dim residual space) and fed each one to kitft/nla-qwen2.5-7b-L20-av in place of a residual-stream activation. Every decode is shown in full below, next to the head's attention pattern and an AR round-trip score.

What came out

  • Most head decodes are confabulation. They are fluent and correctly formatted, but read like the decodes of random Gaussian vectors ("Structured … format with …", invented quotes, nonsense final tokens). The activation reconstructor recovers almost none of the head direction from them: the mean-removed cosine averages 0.032. For the real layer-20 residual at the same tokens it is 0.88, 0.90, 0.78, 0.80.
  • A minority of heads decode to what they attend to, and they do so under both injection styles. L13H16 reads ough+ starter. Its raw decode is about homemade yogurt and bacteria cultures, and its mean+ decode about a homemade yeast culture and “my own sourdough starter”. L2H2 and L22H12 attend to all/-purpose and both decodes keep returning the word “all” (L22H12 once in Chinese, 所有/全部). L10H20 decodes to a girl's letters to her mother, and L22H15 (whose direct logit lens is coastal nights) to an island, a lighthouse and night tides.
  • Heads parked on the first token (the attention sink) give pure noise. That is expected. Their output is close to a fixed vector that carries no information about the context.
  • Head outputs are out of distribution. The median head-output norm is 1.47, against roughly 100–135 for the residual, so the AV has to scale them up 15–300×. They also lack the direction that every residual shares: unit residuals have cosine 0.53 with their mean, while heads sit near 0. The AV never saw vectors like this during training.
  • Adding the mean back helps a little. I injected mean residual + the head's direction, scaled to a typical residual's deviation (“mean+”). Decodes become more fluent, and the AR's reconstruction points at the correct head more often: rank 1 of 10 for 23/40 heads, against 11/40 raw and 4/40 by chance. The cosines stay small (mean 0.052), though, and the mean direction's own content leaks in. My mean came from only 703 tokens, some of them about baking, and it decodes as a baking blog. That is why so many mean+ decodes mention dough, months and buckets.

Setup

Target model and read point
Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in bf16. The NLA reads the output of block 20 (HF hidden_states[21]); I checked my hook against it (exact match).
Head output
For layer l, head h at token t: W_O[:, 128h:128(h+1)] @ z[t, 128h:128(h+1)], where z is the input to o_proj. Qwen2's o_proj has no bias. I checked that the 28 heads sum to the attention block's output. Heads in layers 0–20 write directly into the vector the NLA was trained on. Two heads per text come from layers 21–27, for comparison.
Texts
Four short texts I wrote for this run, so the NLA can't have trained on them: a chat-formatted question, a local-news paragraph, a Rust blog snippet and a piece of fiction. A seeded RNG picked one token per text, then 8 heads from layers 0–20 and 2 from layers 21–27.
Verbalizing
Each vector was rescaled to L2 norm 150 (the sidecar's injection_scale) and written over the ㈎ token embedding in the sidecar's AV prompt. Decoding was greedy, up to 400 new tokens, with HF generate(inputs_embeds=…). Run 1 also drew one sample at T=1 for each raw vector. Greedy decodes were byte-identical across the two runs.
Mean+ variant
Take the head's unit direction, remove its component along the mean unit residual μ (estimated from 703 positions of 9 texts), rescale it to the median off-mean size of a real unit residual (0.83), and add μ back.
Scores
The explanation goes through the AR, giving a reconstruction r. The mean-removed cosine is the cosine between r and the source vector after projecting μ out of both. Without that projection, any residual-shaped reconstruction looks similar to anything that has a μ component. The match rank asks whether r is closer to its own head than to the other 9 heads decoded at the same token.
